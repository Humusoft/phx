phx.RevoluteJoint

Revolute joint

Class  phx.RevoluteJoint  |  Superclass: phx.base.Joint

Description

phx.RevoluteJoint realizes a kinematic constraint with one degree of freedom: a rotation about a shared axis (a hinge). The connection points and the rotation axes are defined in the local space of each body.

The joint can also drive itself. Its motor regulates the joint velocity to TargetVelocity as long as it needs less than MaxTorque, and delivers MaxTorque whenever the target is out of reach. Both properties can be changed while the simulation runs or driven from Simulink.

Creation

Syntax

joint = phx.RevoluteJoint(bodyA,bodyB) joint = phx.RevoluteJoint(___,Name,Value)

Description

joint = phx.RevoluteJoint(bodyA,bodyB) creates a joint between two bodies attached at their origins, with the rotation axes aligned to the local Z axis of each body.

joint = phx.RevoluteJoint(___,Name,Value) sets connection points and axes with name-value arguments (PointA, PointB, AxisA, AxisB).

Input Arguments

ArgumentTypeDescription
bodyA, bodyBphx.Body (scalar)The two bodies connected by the joint.

Properties

PropertyType / DefaultDescription
PointA[0 0 0]Connection point in the local space of body A.
PointB[0 0 0]Connection point in the local space of body B.
AxisA[0 0 1]Rotation-axis direction in the local space of body A.
AxisB[0 0 1]Rotation-axis direction in the local space of body B.
TargetVelocity0Target motor velocity about the joint axis, rad/s. The sign gives the direction of rotation.
MaxTorque0Maximal torque the motor can deliver, N·m. Must be non-negative; 0 means no motor.
OverlayfalseDraw the joint as an overlay (always on top).

Reaction feedback (ForceA, TorqueA, ForceB, TorqueB) and the MutualCollisions switch (collisions between the connected bodies, default false) are inherited from phx.base.Joint.

The joint coordinates Angle and Distance are inherited from there too — the rotation about the joint axis and the offset along it, both measured from the first joint frame to the second. Here Angle is the hinge angle itself, while Distance stays near zero and shows only what the solver leaves of the locked translation.

AxisA/AxisB are the Z axis of the inherited joint frames, so writing those frames another way (EulerAngles*, AxisAngle*, TransformA/TransformB) moves the hinge axis too. The remaining rotation about the axis does not change what the joint constrains — the hinge is handed only its points and axes — but it does move the zero of Angle, which is read between the two joint frames. Roll one frame and the reading shifts with it, so a hinge whose frames start turned against each other reports the angle that actually stands between them.

Driving the joint

The motor is a saturated velocity source, so the two properties cover several kinds of drive without any control law of your own:

To getTargetVelocityMaxTorque
No motor (default)any0
Speed drivethe wanted speed, signedthe torque limit
Pure torque sourcedeliberately out of reach, e.g. sign(tau)*1e6abs(tau)
Friction brake0the holding torque

A speed drive holds its target exactly while it stays under the limit, so nothing needs tuning. A torque source works because the motor cannot reach an impossible target and therefore saturates, delivering the limit and nothing else. A target of zero turns the same mechanism into Coulomb friction: the joint is held until the load exceeds MaxTorque and then slips.

Note. Unlike an external torque applied with phx.Body.applyTorque, the motor is part of the constraint, so the reaction lands on the other body. Driving a wheel hinged to a chassis therefore pushes back on the chassis, which is what a real motor mounted on it does. With two free bodies both turn, in opposite directions, split inversely by their moments of inertia - the motor sets their relative velocity, not the absolute speed of either one.

Examples

Hinge a pendulum to a static base

clf; view(3); axis equal; grid on; camlight headlight;

base = phx.Body("Type", "static", "Position", [0 0 0]);
arm  = phx.Body("Position", [1.5 0 0], "Shape", {"Box", "Size", [3 0.4 0.4]});

phx.RevoluteJoint(base, arm, "PointB", [-1.5 0 0], "AxisA", [0 1 0]);

sim = phx.Simulation;
sim.step(4, 400, 1);
delete(sim);

Spin a disc up with the motor, then brake it

clf; view(3); axis equal; grid on; camlight headlight;

base = phx.Body("Type", "static");
disc = phx.Body("Position", [0 0 0.3], ...
    "Shape", {"Cylinder", "Diameter", 0.4, "Height", 0.1, "Axis", "z"});

hub = phx.RevoluteJoint(base, disc, "PointA", [0 0 0.3], ...
    "TargetVelocity", 6, "MaxTorque", 5);

sim = phx.Simulation;
sim.step(1, 100, 1);                % spins up to the target
disp(disc.AngularVelocity(3))       % 6.00 rad/s

hub.TargetVelocity = 0;             % same motor, now a brake
hub.MaxTorque = 1;
sim.step(2, 200, 1);
disp(disc.AngularVelocity(3))       % 0.00 rad/s
delete(sim);
