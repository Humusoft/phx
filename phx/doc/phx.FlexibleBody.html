phx.FlexibleBody

Deformable solid made of tetrahedra

Class  phx.FlexibleBody  |  Superclass: phx.base.Object

Description

phx.FlexibleBody is a solid of an elastic material given by its Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio: a block of rubber or foam, a soft gripper pad, a bushing, a beam. Its mesh of tetrahedra deforms as a fixed corotated material, which also holds for large rotations; the vertices carry the mass of the tetrahedra around them. It collides with rigid bodies (phx.Body), not with soft or flexible bodies. No joint holds it directly: a body welded into it with phx.IntersectionWeld holds and loads it instead.

The tetrahedra are quadratic: each has a vertex in the middle of each edge, so that it bends without the stiffening of linear tetrahedra on a coarse mesh. A step is solved implicitly by Newton iterations until the force left unbalanced falls below Tolerance, so the stiffness does not depend on Iterations, which only limits the iterations per step; two are usually enough, more only in a step with a sudden load such as an impact.

The body touches rigid bodies at its vertices only: keep the vertices well closer together than the radius of a curved body it touches, or the body feels the spacing of the vertices. The tetrahedra suit large rotations with strains up to some 10-20 %; the material is no calibrated rubber for larger strains.

The mesh is the rest shape, placed at Position and Orientation when the simulation creates the body; the rest shape and the material are read once, at that moment. A flexible body is a top-level object: hand it to the simulation like a body.

Creation

Syntax

flex = phx.FlexibleBody(ax,"Vertices",V,"Tetrahedra",T) flex = phx.FlexibleBody(ax,"Mesh",mesh) flex = phx.FlexibleBody(___,Name,Value)

Description

flex = phx.FlexibleBody(ax,"Vertices",V,"Tetrahedra",T) creates a flexible body of the tetrahedral mesh V (N×3, in the body frame) and T (vertex indices) and draws its surface into the axes ax; [] draws nothing. T is either K×4, as delaunayTriangulation gives it, and the vertices in the middles of the edges are added to V, or K×10, a quadratic mesh (the corners, then the vertices of the edges 12 23 13 14 24 34). The added vertices follow V in CurrentVertices and VonMisesStress.

flex = phx.FlexibleBody(ax,"Mesh",mesh) takes the mesh of tetrahedra that PDE Toolbox generates: the Mesh of an fegeometry or femodel, or the geometry or model itself. PHX only reads the mesh and does not need PDE Toolbox.

Examples

A rubber block dropped on the floor, colored by stress

clf; view(3); axis equal; grid on; camlight headlight;

floor = phx.Body("Type", "static", "Position", [0 0 -0.1], "Shape", {"Box", "Size", [3 3 0.2]});
[x, y, z] = ndgrid(-0.2:0.1:0.2);
V = [x(:) y(:) z(:)];
block = phx.FlexibleBody(gca, "Vertices", V, "Tetrahedra", delaunay(V), ...
    "Position", [0 0 0.6], "YoungsModulus", 2e5, ...
    "Colormap", turbo(64), "ColorRange", [0 2e4]);

sim = phx.Simulation({floor, block});
sim.step(2, 400, 4);

A part meshed by PDE Toolbox

gm = generateMesh(fegeometry(multicuboid(0.4, 0.2, 0.1)), Hmax=0.05);
part = phx.FlexibleBody(gca, "Mesh", gm, "Position", [0 0 0.5], ...
    "YoungsModulus", 1e6);
