phx.engine.Settings

Initial settings of the physics engine

Class  phx.engine.Settings

Description

phx.engine.Settings holds the settings that a phx.Simulation applies to its physics world when it creates it: the tolerances and iteration counts of the constraint solver, the collision tolerances and the threads of the step. Pass the object with the EngineSettings option of the simulation.

The settings take effect once, as the world is created. Changing the object, or the simulation's EngineSettings property, afterwards does not reach a running simulation. Bodies do not fall asleep: the simulation keeps every body active.

Creation

Syntax

s = phx.engine.Settings s = phx.engine.Settings(Name,Value,...)

Description

s = phx.engine.Settings creates the default settings.

s = phx.engine.Settings(Name,Value,...) sets the properties given as name-value pairs.

Properties

PropertyDefaultDescription
JointTolerance1e-3 Velocity tolerance of the joints. The joints of a group of bodies have converged when none of them changed the velocity of one of its bodies in the last iteration by more than this fraction of what the joints and contacts changed it in the step. Smaller is more accurate and takes more iterations; 0 runs every group with a joint to JointIterations, a fixed count. See How the iterations end.
ContactTolerance1e-2 Velocity tolerance of the contacts, as JointTolerance; 0 runs every group to its iteration count. Contacts in heaps converge slowly, hence the looser default.
JointIterations50 The most velocity iterations per step of a group of bodies held together by a joint, and of whatever touches it; fewer once it has converged. Raise it when such groups run to it without meeting their tolerance and the result matters: a hard mechanism roughly halves its joint gap with each doubling, one that does not converge at all gains nothing. 0 leaves such groups at ContactIterations.
ContactIterations20 The most velocity iterations per step of a group of bodies without a joint: contacts, springs and ropes. Heaps rarely converge and run to it, so it sets their cost.
PositionIterations4 Position iterations of the constraint solver per step, which push penetrating bodies and drifted joints back after the velocity iterations. Each takes a fifth of the remaining error: the gap of a joint halves with every doubling, for a few per cent of the step.
LengthScale1 Typical size, in metres, of the parts that touch each other, as an order of magnitude: 1 for parts of about a metre, 0.1 for decimetres, 0.01 for centimetres. When the parts differ much, take the smaller of those whose contacts matter. The collision tolerances in automatic mode follow from it.
Margin0.04 Rounding radius, in metres, of boxes, cylinders, cones and convex hulls, taken inside the shape so that it keeps its size, and limited by its dimensions; also the distance at which concave meshes begin to touch each other. In automatic mode 4 % of LengthScale; setting it switches MarginMode to "manual".
MarginMode"auto" Margin from LengthScale ("auto") or as set ("manual").
PenetrationSlop0.001 Depth, in metres, to which resting bodies may sink into each other before the position iterations push them apart. In automatic mode 0.1 % of LengthScale; setting it switches PenetrationSlopMode to "manual".
PenetrationSlopMode"auto" PenetrationSlop from LengthScale or as set.
SpeculativeDistance0.02 Distance, in metres, ahead of a touch at which contacts begin, so that bodies closing fast do not pass through each other within a step; a larger distance gives more contacts to solve. In automatic mode 2 % of LengthScale; setting it switches SpeculativeDistanceMode to "manual".
SpeculativeDistanceMode"auto" SpeculativeDistance from LengthScale or as set.
Threads1 Threads of the step. 1 runs it on the MATLAB thread alone and suits scenes of up to about a hundred bodies; scenes of hundreds to thousands of bodies, particles or mesh contacts run fastest on about 8. More threads than physical cores usually slow the step down again. The results do not depend on the number of threads.
ContactCachefalse Reuse the contacts of body pairs that moved by less than 1 mm and 2 degrees in a step instead of finding them anew. Faster for large piles at rest, but the reused contacts stay glued to both bodies: slowly rolling or turning bodies gain speed and sliding mesh teeth can wedge.

How the iterations end

The engine solves the joints and contacts of every group of bodies that touch or are joined (an island) iteratively in each step. An island stops iterating when its joints and its contacts have each met their tolerance, and at the latest after JointIterations (an island with a joint) or ContactIterations (any other). A mechanism at rest or moving steadily stops after a few iterations; a load taken up suddenly, a heavy body on a light one or a tall stack runs to the cap.

A tolerance is the relative accuracy of the impulses a body receives: the largest change one joint or contact still made to the body's velocity in the last iteration, as a fraction of what the joints and contacts changed its velocity in the whole step (at least |g| dt). With the default contact tolerance a box sliding on a floor brakes within 0.3 % of its Coulomb friction at any speed. It is the quantity that the diagnostics of the simulation report as JointResidual and ContactResidual (DiagnosticInfo): a residual above the tolerance means that islands ran to their cap (IslandsAtCap), where a higher cap or a shorter step helps; a residual below it with a result still too coarse calls for a smaller tolerance.

The tolerances and caps leave the time step as it is: a step too long for the scene makes stacks creep and joints stretch whatever the iterations (see Units, scale and engine settings).

Examples

Centimetre-sized parts

sim = phx.Simulation(gca, "EngineSettings", ...
    phx.engine.Settings("LengthScale", 0.01));

A precise mechanism

% Tighter joints, and room for more iterations where they need them
opt = phx.engine.Settings("JointTolerance", 1e-4, "JointIterations", 100);
sim = phx.Simulation(gca, "EngineSettings", opt, "DiagnosticInfo", "summary");

A large pile on several threads

opt = phx.engine.Settings("Threads", 8);
sim = phx.Simulation(gca, "EngineSettings", opt);
