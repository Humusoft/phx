Bodies and Shapes

Giving a body its geometry, appearance and physical properties

Body shapes

A shape (Shape) determines how a body looks and how it behaves in collisions. It is given either as a phx.shape.* object, or in shorthand as a cell array {"Box", "Size", …}.

Appearance is tuned with Color, Style (smooth, edged, wireframe, flat), Material and Texture:

clf; view(3); axis equal; grid on; camlight headlight;

phx.Body("Position", [0.5 0 2],   "Shape", {"Sphere", "Style", "wireframe"});
phx.Body("Position", [0 0 0.5],   "Shape", {"Cylinder", "Axis", "y", "Style", "edged"});
phx.Body("Position", [2.5 0 0.5], "Shape", {"Cone", "Axis", "z"});
phx.Body("Position", [2.5 0 2],   "Shape", {"Capsule", "Axis", "z"});
phx.Body("Position", [1 -1.8 1],  "Shape", {"Mesh", "Source", "res/cat.stl", ...
         "Scale", 0.025, "Material", "glossy"});

Body properties and motion

phx.Body is the most important object. Its properties can be both read and written — even during the simulation (they read/write the engine directly).

PropertyMeaning
Position Position [x y z] in global coordinates.
Orientation / EulerAngles / Quaternion Body orientation (rotation matrix, Euler angles or quaternion).
XAxis / YAxis / ZAxis Direction of a body axis. Setting it turns the body by the smallest rotation that points the axis there, handy for shapes with an axis such as a cylinder: "ZAxis", [1 0 1] tilts it 45° without twisting it.
LinearVelocity / AngularVelocity Linear and angular velocity.
Type "dynamic" · "static" · "kinematic". A kinematic body moves only as you drive it. A pose (Position, EulerAngles, …) is reached within the next engine step, and then the body stands still: set it before every step, without substeps (sim.step(dt)), or drive the body by LinearVelocity and AngularVelocity, which hold until changed.
Mass Mass [kg]. Can also be derived from the shape density (Density).
CenterOfMass Centre of mass [m] in the body frame, relative to the origin; the shape gives it, like Mass and Inertia (which is about it). Set it to model a ballasted boat or a loaded vehicle, also while the simulation runs: the body does not move, it only turns about the new centre and gravity acts there.
Friction Coulomb friction coefficient; a contact takes the geometric mean of the values of both bodies.
RollingResistance / SpinRadius Rolling resistance and spin radius [m], both 0 by default: the arm of the normal force that resists rolling, and the effective contact patch radius that resists spinning of a point contact. A contact adds the values of both bodies.
ContactStiffness / ContactDamping Contact stiffness [N/m] and damping [N·s/m]: Inf and 0 by default, a rigid contact. A finite stiffness makes the contacts of the body compliant (see below).
TotalForce / TotalTorque / Energy Current force, torque and kinetic energy (read-only).
LinearAcceleration / AngularAcceleration Mean accelerations over the last step (read-only), in world axes, the linear one of the centre of mass. Gravity counts: a body in free fall reads g, where an accelerometer would read zero. They work for kinematic bodies and bodies without mass as well.

A body can also be acted on directly with a force or a torque. AppliedForce and AppliedTorque stay until changed; applyForce and applyTorque act for the next engine step only, which is one substep of sim.step:

body.AppliedForce = [0 0 50];   % 50 N along the body z axis, in every step
body.AppliedTorque = [0 0 2];   % 2 N*m about the body z axis, in every step
body.applyForce([0 0 50]);      % a 50 N push for one substep
body.LinearVelocity = [2 0 0];  % set velocity at runtime

See Forces, actuators and fields.

Compliant contacts

Contacts are rigid by default: bodies do not sink into each other, and the force of a contact is whatever keeps them apart. A rubber pad, a polyurethane wheel or a padded support is better described by a spring along the contact normal. A finite ContactStiffness k with a ContactDamping c makes every contact of the body a spring-damper, with the force F = max(0, k d + c d′) of the penetration d. It is the contact law of Simscape Multibody's Spatial Contact Force and of Adams, in the same units:

  • A body resting on a support sinks F/k into it, and a gripper pad pressed in by d clamps with k d.
  • The contact never pulls. Restitution does not apply; the damping ratio c/(2√(km)) of a mass m sets the rebound instead.
  • Friction, RollingResistance and SpinRadius act as on a rigid contact.
  • The two bodies act in series, 1/k = 1/k1 + 1/k2 (the damping likewise): a compliant body keeps its own values against a rigid one, so it is compliant against everything it touches.
pad = phx.Body("Type", "kinematic", "Shape", {"Box", "Size", [0.04 0.3 0.3]}, ...
    "ContactStiffness", 1e4, "ContactDamping", 70);   % 10 N per mm, rubber
pad.TotalForce                  % the contact force on the pad, after a step

A body of mass m stays in contact for about Tc = π√(m/k). Resting, rolling and clamping contacts come out right at any step. The force and the rebound of an impact follow the law within a few per cent with an engine step of Tc/10 or less, and within about 10 % up to Tc/3. With coarser steps impacts become inelastic, while the contact stays stable even at a very high stiffness. A stiffness that leaves a penetration of a millimetre or more under the working loads is usually what a compliant contact is for; keep the penetration well below the size of the shapes.

Where it helps. In phxex_grip the jaw pads clamp the object with ContactStiffness times their squeeze, and the plot shows the clamping force and the friction carrying the object; rigid jaws pressed in by the same 3.5 mm clamped it with kilonewtons. In phxex_coaster compliant wheel treads take the knocks of the faceted rails: the wheels stay on the rails, and the train loses less speed.

The stiffness belongs to the whole contact face and is shared by its points, so a box on its face rocks as on springs at its corners. A body touching a triangle mesh may meet it in several faces at once, and is then stiffer than its ContactStiffness says. Soft bodies, flexible bodies and particles keep rigid contacts.

Coordinate system. PHX uses the z axis pointing up by default; the default gravity is [0 0 -9.81] m/s². You change it when creating the simulation: phx.Simulation("Gravity", [0 0 0]) for weightlessness.
