Units, Scale and Engine Settings

Choosing the time step, matching the engine to the size of the parts, checking the accuracy, and running on several threads

Units

PHX works in SI units: metres, kilograms, seconds, newtons, radians. The z axis points up and gravity is [0 0 -9.81] m/s² unless you set the Gravity of the simulation. Model parts at their real size: a scene scaled up to make small parts easier to simulate also hides real problems, such as springs too soft for the loads they carry.

Time step

The physics engine advances the scene in fixed substeps, interval/substeps of sim.step(interval, substeps). The substep is the most important setting for accuracy and stability: a step too long makes stacks creep, joints stretch and fast bodies pass through thin walls, and more solver iterations do not make up for it.

SceneSubstep to start with
Bodies of about a metre, loose contacts, vehicles5–10 ms
Joint networks, stiff springs, stacks, mechanisms in contact5 ms or less
Parts of a few centimetres, gears, ratchets1 ms or less

Smaller parts need shorter steps: their motions are faster in proportion to their size. Treat the table as a starting point and check a result that matters by halving the substep: if the result changes, the step was too long.

Length scale and collision tolerances

The engine keeps small tolerances around every contact: shapes are slightly rounded (Margin), resting bodies may sink into each other by a tiny depth (PenetrationSlop), and contacts begin a little before bodies touch (SpeculativeDistance). By default these suit parts of about a metre. For smaller or larger parts, set one value, the LengthScale of phx.engine.Settings: the typical size of the parts whose contacts matter, as an order of magnitude. The tolerances then follow from it.

PartsLengthScaleExample
About a metre: furniture, machines, large boxes1 (default)phxex_drum
Decimetres: wheels of a small vehicle, bricks, gravel0.1phxex_buggy, phxex_soilparticles
Centimetres: small parts, dice, marbles0.01phxex_bowlfeeder
opt = phx.engine.Settings("LengthScale", 0.01);   % centimetre parts
sim = phx.Simulation(gca, "EngineSettings", opt);
sim.step(2, 2000);                                   % and 1 ms substeps

When the parts differ much in size, take the smaller of those whose contacts matter. Too large a scale makes small parts float on rounded edges and slows them down with needless contacts; too small a scale lets fast bodies pass through each other. Any of the three tolerances can also be set directly, which overrides the value derived from the scale.

Solver iterations

Contacts and joints are solved iteratively in every substep, each group of bodies that touch or are joined on its own. A group stops iterating once its joints and contacts are accurate enough: JointTolerance (default 10−3) and ContactTolerance (default 10−2) of phx.engine.Settings are the relative accuracy of the impulses a body receives. A mechanism at rest or in steady motion needs only a few iterations; a group that has not converged stops at JointIterations (default 50, a group with a joint) or ContactIterations (default 20, any other).

The defaults suit most scenes. For a mechanism that must be more precise, lower JointTolerance; when the diagnostics below report groups at their cap, raise the cap. Tall stacks and heavy bodies resting on light ones converge slowly at any setting: mass ratios beyond about 1:100 between touching bodies are hard for the solver. If more iterations do not help, shorten the substep instead. PositionIterations (default 4) pulls drifted joints and penetrating bodies back after the velocity iterations.

Checking the accuracy

Whether the substep, the length scale and the iterations suit a scene can be checked while it runs. Set DiagnosticInfo of the simulation, and the engine reports after every step how far its solver was from converging, how deep bodies sink into each other, how far joints pull apart and how fast the bodies move. It warns when a value is beyond what the scene can be trusted with, and names the remedy.

sim = phx.Simulation(gca, "DiagnosticInfo", "summary");
sim.step(1, 200);                    % one line per call, warnings when needed
r = sim.getDiagnosticReport;         % the numbers of the last call, as a struct

Use "warnings" to be told only about problems, "summary" for one line per call of step, and "detailed" to see which bodies and joints are concerned. Turn the diagnostics off again once the scene is tuned: they cost a few per cent of the engine time. The levels, the warnings and the fields of the report are described in phx.Simulation.

Threads

Threads runs the step of the engine on several threads. The results do not depend on the number of threads: a run repeats bit for bit on one thread or on eight. Whether more threads pay off depends on the amount of work in a step:

SceneThreads
Up to about a hundred bodies1 (default); more threads only add overhead
Hundreds to thousands of bodies, particles, mesh contactsabout 8; typically 3–6 times faster
A single soft or flexible body1; its solver gains little from threads

More threads than physical cores, or than one group of cores of the processor shares a cache, usually make the step slower again; try a few counts on your computer. Only the engine runs on the threads: with thousands of moving bodies the MATLAB side of the step, which updates the phx.* objects and the graphics, soon takes the larger part of the time.

opt = phx.engine.Settings("Threads", 8);
sim = phx.Simulation(gca, "EngineSettings", opt);
Settings take effect once. The simulation applies its EngineSettings when it creates the physics world. Changing them afterwards does not reach a running simulation; create a new one.
