phx.engine.BulletSettings

Initial settings of the Bullet physics engine

Class  phx.engine.BulletSettings

Description

phx.engine.BulletSettings holds the settings that a phx.Simulation applies to its physics world when it creates it: the constraint solver and its iterations, body sleeping and the collision margin. Pass the object with the EngineSettings option of the simulation.

The settings take effect once, as the world is created. Changing the object, or the simulation's EngineSettings property, afterwards does not reach a running simulation.

Creation

Syntax

s = phx.engine.BulletSettings s = phx.engine.BulletSettings(Name,Value,...)

Description

s = phx.engine.BulletSettings creates the default settings.

s = phx.engine.BulletSettings(Name,Value,...) sets the properties given as name-value pairs.

Properties

PropertyDefaultDescription
Solver"sequentialimpulse" Constraint solver: "sequentialimpulse", "nncg", or the MLCP solvers "dantzig", "lemke" and "gaussseidelprojected". The choice matters in coupled systems such as joint chains and stacks of bodies; see Solvers.
Iterations10 Iterations of the constraint solver per step, a positive integer. More iterations make joint chains and stacks stiffer, at a cost that grows in proportion. The direct solvers "dantzig" and "lemke" do not use them. Iterations do not help when the time step itself is too long; then lower the step.
AutoActivatedfalse Let resting bodies fall asleep (true) or keep every body active (false). A sleeping body is woken by a direct interaction (a force, a velocity or pose write, a new contact), but not by a change of its surroundings, such as gravity or a support removed from under it. Sleeping saves little time in most scenes, so the default is usually the right choice.
Margin0.04 Collision margin, in metres, added around convex hull, concave mesh and cone collision shapes. Boxes, spheres, cylinders and capsules keep their exact size. Lower it for small objects, for which the default margin is a visible gap.

Solvers

In every step the constraint solver finds the impulses that keep joints together, stop bodies from sinking into each other and apply friction and motor limits. The solvers differ in how they find them. None of them chooses the time step: PHX advances the engine by exactly the step given to phx.Simulation, so a step that is too long cannot be made up for by the solver.

SolverDescription
"sequentialimpulse" The default. An iterative solver that corrects one constraint at a time and repeats the sweep Iterations times. It is fast and scales to many bodies and contacts, but long joint chains, tall stacks and heavy bodies on light links converge slowly and stay slightly soft. More iterations make them stiffer.
"nncg" An iterative solver of the same kind, using nonlinear conjugate gradients to converge in fewer iterations. It can diverge in scenes with large mass ratios, so check the motion when you use it.
"dantzig" A direct solver: it assembles all constraints of a group of touching or jointed bodies into one system and solves it exactly by pivoting, so joints do not pull apart even in long chains with heavy parts. It ignores Iterations. Its cost grows steeply with the number of constraints, so it suits small mechanisms such as arms and linkages, not piles of contacting bodies. A step it cannot solve falls back to "sequentialimpulse".
"lemke" A direct solver like "dantzig", based on a different pivoting algorithm. It is slower and treats friction less faithfully, as its friction limits do not follow the normal force; prefer "dantzig".
"gaussseidelprojected" The iterative method of "sequentialimpulse" run on the assembled system of the direct solvers. It gives the same results, only more slowly, and is there for comparison.

Examples

Small parts with a thin collision margin

sim = phx.Simulation(gca, "EngineSettings", ...
    phx.engine.BulletSettings("Margin", 0.002));

Another solver for a tall stack

opt = phx.engine.BulletSettings("Solver", "dantzig");
sim = phx.Simulation(gca, "EngineSettings", opt);
