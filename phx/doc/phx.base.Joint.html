phx.base.Joint

Superclass for custom kinematic joints

Abstract class  phx.base.Joint  |  Superclass: phx.base.Object

Description

phx.base.Joint is the abstract superclass for kinematic constraints connecting two bodies, referred to as body A and body B. Concrete joints derive from it and define the actual constraint; this class provides what all of them share: the joint frames TransformA and TransformB together with the helpers that read and write parts of them, the reaction force and torque feedback (ForceA, TorqueA, ForceB, TorqueB), and the MutualCollisions switch controlling whether the two connected bodies collide with each other.

The axis of the free degrees of freedom is always the Z axis of the joint frames — the rotation axis of a phx.RevoluteJoint or a phx.GearJoint, the sliding axis of a phx.PrismaticJoint, both at once for a phx.CylindricalJoint — and AxisA/AxisB read and write exactly that axis, as the third column of the frame. Setting one leaves the frame otherwise as intact as it can: the connection point is untouched, and the rotation about the axis is kept unless the axis moves close to where the frame's local X axis used to point.

Every joint inherits the whole set, but each one reads only the part its constraint needs. The rest stays settable and simply has no effect — setting an axis on a ball joint, or a connection point on a gear coupling, changes nothing and is not reported as an error:

JointReadsNo effect
phx.RevoluteJoint PointA/PointB, AxisA/AxisB the rotation about the axis, i.e. the part of the frame rotation that AxisA/AxisB do not determine
phx.CylindricalJoint PointA/PointB, AxisA/AxisB the rotation about the axis, and an offset of PointA/PointB along it — both degrees of freedom are free
phx.SphericalJoint PointA/PointB AxisA/AxisB, frame rotation
phx.GearJoint AxisA/AxisB PointA/PointB, the rotation about the axis
phx.PrismaticJoint the full frames, i.e. AxisA/AxisB and the rotation about the axis an offset of PointA/PointB along the sliding axis
phx.FixedJoint, phx.BushingJoint,
phx.GenericJoint		 the full frames TransformA/TransformB, and so every helper that writes into them nothing — these joints have no single free axis, so AxisA/AxisB just move the frame's Z axis like any other helper
Note. AxisA/AxisB fully describe a joint only where the rotation about the axis is free. A phx.PrismaticJoint locks it, so its two frames must also agree on where their remaining axes point — which the axis alone does not say. Setting only the axes on a prismatic joint whose bodies do not share an orientation can leave the frames rolled against each other about the sliding axis, and the solver then twists that out. Give such a joint its TransformA/TransformB, or the EulerAngles helpers, in full.

A joint keeps both joint frames in coincidence, except for the degrees of freedom it leaves free (the rotation of a revolute joint, the translation of a prismatic one, none at all for a fixed joint). Set the frames consistently with the initial poses of the bodies: a mismatch in a constrained direction is not reported as an error, the solver simply pulls the bodies together over the following steps. A mismatch in a free direction is harmless and only shifts the zero of that degree of freedom, for example an offset of PointA along the sliding axis of a phx.PrismaticJoint.

Examples

Record joint reaction forces with a logger

clf; view(3); axis equal; grid on; camlight headlight;

A = phx.Body("Type", "static", "Position", [1 1 0]);
B = phx.Body("Position", [4 1 0]);
C = phx.Body("Position", [4 -2 0]);

phx.RevoluteJoint(A, B, "PointA", [1.5 0 0], "PointB", [-1.5 0 0]);
S = phx.SphericalJoint(B, C, "PointA", [0 -1.5 0], "PointB", [0 1.5 0]);

log = phx.Logger(S, "Frequency", 100, "Parameters", ["ForceA" "ForceB"]);

sim = phx.Simulation;
sim.step(6, 600, 1);
delete(sim);

plot(log.Time, log.getChannel(1));     % reaction force on body A
