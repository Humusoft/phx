phx.PrismaticJoint

Prismatic joint

Class  phx.PrismaticJoint  |  Superclass: phx.base.Joint

Description

phx.PrismaticJoint realizes a kinematic constraint with one degree of freedom: a translation along a shared sliding axis. The sliding axis is the local Z axis of the joint frame of each connected body; the remaining five degrees of freedom are locked, so the bodies keep their relative orientation and slide without rotating.

The joint can also drive itself. Its motor regulates the sliding velocity to TargetVelocity as long as it needs less than MaxForce, and delivers MaxForce whenever the target is out of reach. Both properties can be changed while the simulation runs or driven from Simulink.

Creation

Syntax

joint = phx.PrismaticJoint(bodyA,bodyB) joint = phx.PrismaticJoint(___,Name,Value)

Description

joint = phx.PrismaticJoint(bodyA,bodyB) creates a joint between two bodies attached at their origins, with the sliding axis aligned to the local Z axis of each body. Set custom sliding axes with AxisA and AxisB, custom joint frames with TransformA and TransformB, or the connection points and orientations with PointA, PointB, EulerAnglesA and EulerAnglesB; in every case the sliding axis is the local Z axis of each frame. Only the components perpendicular to the sliding axis place the bodies with respect to each other; an offset along the sliding axis merely shifts the zero of the free translation.

Reaction feedback (ForceA, TorqueA, ForceB, TorqueB) and the MutualCollisions switch (collisions between the connected bodies, default false) are inherited from phx.base.Joint.

The joint coordinates Angle and Distance are inherited from there too — the rotation about the joint axis and the offset along it, both measured from the first joint frame to the second. Here Distance is the travel itself, while Angle stays near zero and shows only what the solver leaves of the locked rotation.

Note. This joint locks the rotation about the sliding axis as well, so AxisA/AxisB do not fully describe it: they fix where each frame points its Z axis, but not how the two frames are rolled about it. They are enough when both bodies share an orientation, as a body and a part sliding straight out of it usually do. When they do not, set TransformA and TransformB (or the EulerAngles helpers) so that the frames coincide in every axis — otherwise the solver twists the bodies into line over the first steps.

Driving the joint

The motor is a saturated velocity source, so the two properties cover several kinds of drive without any control law of your own:

To getTargetVelocityMaxForce
No motor (default)any0
Speed drivethe wanted speed, signedthe force limit
Pure force sourcedeliberately out of reach, e.g. sign(f)*1e6abs(f)
Friction brake0the holding force

A speed drive holds its target exactly while it stays under the limit, so nothing needs tuning. A force source works because the motor cannot reach an impossible target and therefore saturates, delivering the limit and nothing else. A target of zero turns the same mechanism into Coulomb friction: the joint is held until the load exceeds MaxForce and then slips, at the acceleration the surplus load is worth.

Note. MaxForce is a force in newtons, not a fraction of anything, so size it against the load the joint actually carries — a body of a cubic metre of default density already weighs a tonne. Like the motor of a phx.RevoluteJoint, this one is part of the constraint, so its reaction lands on the other body: a carriage driven along a rail pushes back on whatever holds the rail.

Examples

Bounce a slider down an inclined rail

clf; view(3); axis equal; grid on; camlight headlight;

base   = phx.Body("Type", "static", "Restitution", 0.5);
slider = phx.Body("Position", [-4 0 4], "EulerAngles", [0 pi/4 0], "Restitution", 1);

% the sliding axis is the local Z of the joint frame; tilting it makes an
% inclined rail down which the slider slides and rebounds off the base.
% Both frames are given, as the slider itself is already turned by pi/4 and
% a prismatic joint locks the rotation about the rail too
phx.PrismaticJoint(base, slider, "EulerAnglesA", [0 3*pi/4 0], "EulerAnglesB", [0 pi/2 0], "MutualCollisions", true);

sim = phx.Simulation;
sim.step(2, 200, 1);
delete(sim);

Raise a lift car with the motor, then hold it there

clf; view(3); axis equal; grid on; camlight headlight;

base = phx.Body("Type", "static");
car  = phx.Body("Position", [0 0 0.5], ...
    "Shape", {"Box", "Size", [0.6 0.6 0.4]});   % 144 kg, 1413 N

rail = phx.PrismaticJoint(base, car, "TargetVelocity", 0.5, "MaxForce", 4e3);

sim = phx.Simulation;
sim.step(2, 200, 1);            % climbs at 0.5 m/s against its own weight
disp(rail.Distance)             % 1.50 - the 0.5 m it started at plus 1 m travelled

rail.TargetVelocity = 0;        % the same motor, now a brake holding the car up
sim.step(2, 200, 1);
disp(rail.Distance)             % 1.50 still, and the car is at rest
delete(sim);
